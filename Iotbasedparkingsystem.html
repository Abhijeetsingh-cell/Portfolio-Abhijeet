Smart Traffic Control System

A working prototype that senses vehicles at each lane of an intersection and adjusts signal timing automatically, instead of running on a fixed clock — built to cut needless waiting at low-traffic hours.

Role
Hardware & firmware
Stack
NodeMCU (ESP8266), C++
Duration
3 weeks
Status
Prototype, bench-tested
01

The problem

Most traffic signals run on a fixed cycle regardless of how many cars are actually waiting. Late at night or during off-peak hours, that means full stops at empty intersections — wasted time and unnecessary fuel burn.

I wanted to test whether a low-cost sensor setup could make that timing responsive to real conditions, using hardware simple enough to prototype on a breadboard.

Goal: detect vehicle presence per lane in real time and shift green-light duration toward the lane that actually needs it.

02

How it works

  1. Sense

    IR obstacle sensors mounted at each lane detect whether a vehicle is present, feeding digital signals back to the controller in real time.

  2. Decide

    The NodeMCU reads all lane states and runs the timing logic — lanes with vehicles waiting get priority; empty lanes are skipped or shortened.

  3. Display

    A 16x2 I2C LCD shows the current signal status and countdown, so the logic is visible and easy to debug on the bench.

  4. Actuate

    A servo motor stands in for a physical signal or barrier arm, and an LED strip simulates the light sequence across the model intersection.

What's next

The bench version proves the logic works. Next step is moving from toy-car simulation to a scaled physical model with all four lanes wired, then testing WiFi reporting so signal states can be logged and viewed remotely.

Longer term, I'm curious about swapping the IR sensors for a small camera + lightweight vision model, to count vehicles rather than just detect presence.
